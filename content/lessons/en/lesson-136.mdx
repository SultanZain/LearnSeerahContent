## Returning to a Topic First Introduced in Unit 21

We first encountered the revelation of verses regarding hijab in Unit 21, within our examination of the marriage to Zaynab bint Jahsh <span className="font-ar">رضي الله عنها</span> and the broader household life of the Prophet ﷺ. This lesson returns to this topic to provide the fuller and more complete treatment its significance deserves.

{/* page */}

## The Historical Context of This Revelation

As we examined in Unit 21, these verses were revealed during a period when the Prophet's ﷺ household had grown considerably through the various marriages examined throughout that unit — Sawdah, Aisha, Hafsah, Zaynab bint Khuzaymah, Umm Salamah, Zaynab bint Jahsh, and the marriages of reconciliation that followed. As this household expanded and as the broader Muslim community's attention toward the Prophet's ﷺ wives naturally increased, a genuine social need emerged for clear guidance regarding appropriate boundaries of interaction and modest conduct.

## The Quran's Stated Purpose

Surah al-Ahzab addresses this matter directly, and importantly, states the underlying purpose behind this guidance with clarity:

<Quran surah="33" ayah="59" />

*"O Prophet, tell your wives and your daughters and the women of the believers to bring down over themselves part of their outer garments. That is more suitable that they will be known and not be abused. And ever is Allah Forgiving and Merciful."*

This verse establishes explicitly that the purpose of this guidance is protective — enabling believing women to be recognized as such and treated with appropriate respect and dignity, distinguishing their conduct from behavior that might expose them to harassment or harm in the specific social context of that time and place. This is not, in the Quran's own stated framing, an arbitrary restriction, but a measure designed with the safety and dignity of women specifically in mind.

## Connecting to a Broader Pattern of Protection

This legislation connects directly and meaningfully to the broader pattern this curriculum has traced since our examination of pre-Islamic Arabian society in Section 4, where we documented the genuine vulnerability and lack of protection women faced within that social structure — including the practices of female infanticide and the exclusion from inheritance that we examined in that section and revisited in the previous lesson of this unit.

Considered alongside the inheritance rights we just examined, and the broader Quranic emphasis on protecting the vulnerable that has recurred throughout nearly every unit of this entire section — from the institution of Zakah, through the general amnesty at Mecca, to the specific protections established for widows and orphans — this guidance regarding modest dress forms part of a coherent and consistent Quranic framework addressing the dignity and protection of women as a central and recurring theme of the entire Islamic legal architecture established throughout the Medinan period.

## Acknowledging Genuine Scholarly Diversity

Consistent with the evenhanded approach this curriculum has applied to other complex legal and social topics throughout this unit, it is appropriate to acknowledge honestly that the specific practical application of this guidance — precisely what garments, what degree of covering, and in what specific social contexts — has been the subject of genuine, sincere, and extensive scholarly discussion across different schools of Islamic jurisprudence throughout the fourteen centuries since this guidance was first revealed.

This curriculum does not present itself as adjudicating between these various sincere scholarly traditions, each rooted in careful study of the relevant texts and their broader context. What this curriculum presents is the core Quranic guidance itself, its clearly stated protective purpose, and its place within the broader and coherent framework of Islamic teaching regarding the dignity and protection of women — a framework this entire section has traced consistently since its earliest units.
