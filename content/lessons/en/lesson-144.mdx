## A Different Kind of Illness

Not long after returning from the profound and historic experience of the Farewell Pilgrimage examined throughout the previous unit, the Prophet ﷺ fell ill — an illness that would, over the course of the following weeks, prove to be his last.

{/* page */}

## The Onset of Symptoms

The illness began with fever and headache, symptoms that the companions رضي الله عنهم present recognized, as the days progressed, as genuinely different in character and severity from any illness the Prophet ﷺ had previously experienced throughout his life. This gradual intensification, rather than a sudden and immediately incapacitating onset, gave the final weeks of his life a particular quality — allowing time for the deliberate teaching, instruction, and preparation that would characterize this final period.

## Continuing His Responsibilities as Long as Possible

Consistent with the character this curriculum has traced throughout his entire life — the man who worked alongside his companions رضي الله عنهم in building both the mosque in Medina and the trench before the confederate siege, examined in earlier units of this section — the Prophet ﷺ continued, for as long as his physical strength genuinely permitted, to lead the congregational prayers and attend personally to matters of concern to the Muslim community.

Only as his condition gradually worsened, and his physical strength diminished beyond what continued personal leadership of these responsibilities could sustain, did he begin, with evident reluctance and genuine physical struggle, to delegate these duties to others.

## The Instruction Regarding Abu Bakr رضي الله عنه

As his illness progressed to the point where he could no longer lead the congregational prayers personally, the Prophet ﷺ gave a specific and significant instruction: that Abu Bakr <span className="font-ar">رضي الله عنه</span> should lead the prayers in his place.

This decision carried weight that many companions رضي الله عنهم present, and subsequent generations of Islamic scholars, have understood as significant given everything this curriculum has traced regarding Abu Bakr's <span className="font-ar">رضي الله عنه</span> unique position throughout the entire history of the mission — his immediate and unwavering acceptance of Islam on the very night of the first revelation examined in Section 4, his steadfast companionship through the cave of Thawr during the Hijrah examined in Section 5, and his consistent presence at nearly every significant moment this entire curriculum has traced.

Aisha <span className="font-ar">رضي الله عنها</span>, understanding the emotional weight this specific instruction might carry for her own father, reportedly attempted to suggest an alternative arrangement, expressing concern for how difficult this particular responsibility might prove for him during such an emotionally overwhelming period. The Prophet ﷺ, however, maintained his instruction, insisting that Abu Bakr <span className="font-ar">رضي الله عنه</span> should lead the prayers.

## Cared for Within Aisha's رضي الله عنها Quarters

Aisha <span className="font-ar">رضي الله عنها</span> herself preserved a detailed and deeply moving account of this period, connecting directly to the genuine and personal household life this curriculum examined with such care throughout Unit 21.

As his illness progressed, the Prophet ﷺ, wishing to know where he might rest most peacefully during his suffering, asked his wives directly where he would be permitted to spend his time. With the gracious and immediate agreement of his other wives, he came to spend his final days within Aisha's <span className="font-ar">رضي الله عنها</span> own quarters — the same quarters, adjoining the mosque he had built with his own hands, that we examined in detail when studying the Prophet's Mosque in Unit 13 of Section 5.

Aisha <span className="font-ar">رضي الله عنها</span> cared for him personally throughout this period, her narrations preserving details of his final days with the same intimate and careful attention that had characterized her preservation of so much of the Sunnah this curriculum has drawn upon throughout its entire treatment of his household life.

## What Remained Ahead

As this illness continued to progress, and as the Prophet's ﷺ physical strength gradually diminished, one particular sound continued to mark the rhythm of daily life within the mosque and the broader Muslim community — the call to prayer, given as it had been since the earliest days in Medina by the same voice that had once called it from atop the conquered Kaaba itself.

The specific and deeply moving account of Bilal's <span className="font-ar">رضي الله عنه</span> final calls during this period, and what they reveal about the emotional weight of this approaching conclusion for the entire Muslim community, forms the subject of our next lesson.
