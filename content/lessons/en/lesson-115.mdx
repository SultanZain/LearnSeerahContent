## A Long-Standing Threat Finally Addressed

The relative peace secured through the Treaty of Hudaybiyyah, examined extensively throughout the previous units of this section, created the strategic conditions that allowed the Prophet ﷺ to turn his attention toward a threat that had been building for years: the fortified oasis settlement of Khaybar.

{/* page */}

## Banu al-Nadir's Continuing Hostility

We examined in Unit 18 how Banu al-Nadir, following their expulsion from Medina after the discovered assassination plot against the Prophet ﷺ, resettled primarily at Khaybar. Rather than accepting their exile peacefully, their leadership, particularly Huyay ibn Akhtab, used their new base and considerable resources to actively incite and help organize the massive confederate coalition against Medina — the very crisis that occupied Unit 18 and Unit 19 of this section, culminating in the extended siege we examined in such detail.

This pattern of ongoing hostility did not end with the coalition's dispersal following the storm we examined in Unit 19. Khaybar's leadership continued to represent a genuine and documented security concern for the Muslim community, a persistent threat requiring resolution.

## Why This Was the Right Moment

The specific timing of this campaign, occurring after the Treaty of Hudaybiyyah had secured the Muslim community's relationship with Quraysh through the ten-year truce we examined in detail in Unit 23, reflects careful strategic consideration.

With the southern and western threat from Quraysh substantially neutralized through this negotiated peace, the Prophet ﷺ could direct sustained military attention toward Khaybar without the considerable risk of facing simultaneous conflict on multiple fronts — precisely the kind of dangerous, multi-directional threat that had made the crisis of the Trench, examined in Unit 19, so genuinely severe. This sequencing reflects the same pattern of careful, deliberate strategic planning that has characterized nearly every major decision we have examined throughout this entire section.

## A Fortified and Challenging Objective

Khaybar presented a considerably different military challenge than the engagements we have previously examined throughout this section. Rather than a single open-field confrontation, as had characterized Badr and Uhud, or a prolonged siege against a unified position, as had characterized the Trench and the subsequent resolution with Banu Qurayza, Khaybar consisted of a series of separate and genuinely well-constructed fortresses spread across the oasis settlement.

This meant the Muslim forces needed to address each fortified position individually — a more prolonged, complex, and militarily demanding undertaking than several of the engagements this curriculum has already examined in detail. The campaign required patience, careful tactical planning, and sustained effort across multiple distinct engagements rather than a single decisive confrontation.

## A Campaign of Genuine Significance

The campaign at Khaybar represents an important chapter in the broader story of this period — not merely as a military engagement, but as the resolution of a threat that had persisted, in various forms, since the exile of Banu al-Nadir years earlier. The outcome of this campaign, and the significant developments that followed in its aftermath — including the marriage to Safiyyah <span className="font-ar">رضي الله عنها</span> that we already anticipated in our discussion of marriages of reconciliation in Unit 21, and a serious and documented assassination attempt against the Prophet ﷺ himself — form the subject of the remaining lessons in this unit.
