## A Commander's Warning Unheeded

As we saw in the previous lesson, the Prophet's ﷺ instruction to the archers stationed on the hill had been explicit and repeated: hold this position regardless of how the battle appeared to be progressing, whether the Muslims seemed to be winning or losing, until a direct command instructed otherwise.

Abdullah ibn Jubayr <span className="font-ar">رضي الله عنه</span>, commanding this group, attempted to hold his men to this instruction as the perception of victory spread across the battlefield and many began preparing to descend toward the abandoned spoils.

*"Have you forgotten what the Messenger of Allah told us?"*

His reminder was not without effect on everyone — a small number remained with him, recognizing the binding nature of the original instruction. But the majority, convinced that the battle's outcome was already settled and that further vigilance served no remaining purpose, proceeded to leave the hill regardless.

## Khalid ibn al-Walid Sees His Opening

Among the commanders leading portions of Quraysh's cavalry that day was Khalid ibn al-Walid — a man whose name would, in the years ahead, become synonymous with extraordinary military skill once he accepted Islam and fought on behalf of the very community he opposed at Uhud.

Khalid possessed the kind of tactical awareness that recognized, immediately, what the now largely undefended hill represented: a clear path around the Muslim position's previously protected rear. He led his cavalry in a decisive maneuver around the hill and into the space the departed archers had left exposed.

This was not a fortunate accident for Quraysh, nor a stroke of luck disconnected from any specific cause. It was a direct and immediate exploitation of the specific gap that had opened the moment the archers abandoned the position they had been explicitly instructed to hold.

## A Battle on Two Fronts

The effect of this maneuver was immediate and severe. The Muslim forces, who only moments before had been pressing forward against a retreating enemy and beginning to perceive the battle as decisively won, suddenly found themselves under attack from the rear as well as facing a front line that, sensing this reversal, began to regroup and press forward once again.

This combination produced genuine and understandable confusion within the Muslim ranks. Soldiers oriented toward what they believed was a victorious forward engagement now faced an unexpected threat from behind, with the clear lines of the battle dissolving into a more chaotic and dangerous engagement on multiple fronts simultaneously.

The narrations describe a period of intense disorder, during which soldiers struggled to identify friend from foe amid the rising dust and confusion, and in which some of the most painful losses of the entire battle — which we will examine in the lessons ahead — began to occur.

## The Quran's Direct Account

The Quran addresses this specific reversal with a clarity that leaves little room for alternative explanation, situating the cause precisely where the events themselves point:

<Quran surah="3" ayah="152" />

*And Allah had certainly fulfilled His promise to you when you were killing them by His permission, until you lost courage and fell into disagreement about the order and disobeyed after He had shown you that for which you long. Among you are some who desire this world, and among you are some who desire the Hereafter. Then He turned you back from them that He might test you. And He has already forgiven you, and Allah is bearing of grace to the believers.*

This verse does something important that deserves careful attention: it explicitly affirms that Allah's promise of support had indeed been fulfilled in the battle's earlier phase — the genuine success we examined in the previous lesson was real, attributed directly to divine permission and support. The reversal that followed is attributed specifically to disagreement and disobedience regarding the instruction, occurring once the desired outcome appeared to have already been achieved.

## A Mercy Within the Hardship

It is worth noting something easily overlooked amid the difficulty of this passage: the verse concludes with an explicit statement of forgiveness — *"He has already forgiven you"* — extended toward those whose departure from the instruction had contributed to the reversal.

This combination of honest acknowledgment of error alongside genuine forgiveness reflects a pattern we have seen consistently throughout this curriculum. The Quran does not shy away from identifying specific human failings with precision, even regarding sincere believers who had fought bravely only moments before. But the identification of error exists alongside, not in place of, Allah's mercy toward those who had erred — a mercy that did not erase the consequences of the moment, but did not condemn those involved to permanent blame either.

This balance matters considerably as we proceed through the remainder of this difficult day, which would bring further hardship before its conclusion — including the personal grief of losing Hamzah <span className="font-ar">رضي الله عنه</span>, whose martyrdom and the circumstances surrounding it we examine directly in our next lesson.

## What Remained Ahead

With the Muslim formation now disrupted and fighting on two fronts simultaneously, the battle entered its most difficult and costly phase. The disciplined advantage that had characterized the engagement's opening hour had given way to chaos, and the Prophet ﷺ himself would soon face direct and serious danger as the fighting intensified around him.

The events that followed — the martyrdom of Hamzah <span className="font-ar">رضي الله عنه</span>, the injury to the Prophet ﷺ himself, and the remarkable courage shown by those who rallied to protect him in the midst of this confusion — form the subject of the lessons that follow, as we continue to walk through this most consequential and instructive day in the early history of the Muslim community.
