## A Routine Visit, An Extraordinary Plot

In the months following the difficult events at Uhud that we examined throughout the previous unit, an incident occurred involving Banu al-Nadir — one of the Jewish tribes of Medina, like Banu Qaynuqa a signatory to the Constitution we studied carefully in Section 5 — that would prove to be one of the most serious breaches of trust the young Medinan community had yet faced.

The occasion for this incident began in entirely ordinary circumstances. A matter of blood money — financial compensation owed in connection with an earlier incident — required resolution, and the Constitution specifically designated the Prophet ﷺ as the arbiter for exactly this kind of dispute between the city's different communities, as we examined in detail in Section 5. He traveled to the quarter of Banu al-Nadir, accompanied by several companions رضي الله عنهم, for what should have been a routine and peaceful negotiation.

## A Plot Forms Above His Head

As the discussion proceeded, with the Prophet ﷺ seated with his back against a wall of one of the tribe's buildings, members of Banu al-Nadir began quietly conspiring among themselves. The plan they formed was direct and unambiguous: someone would climb to the rooftop directly above where he sat and drop a heavy stone, sufficient to kill him, while he remained unaware and engaged in the discussion below.

This was not an act of impulsive passion arising from a sudden dispute, as had been the case with the marketplace incident that triggered the conflict with Banu Qaynuqa. This was calculated, premeditated, and specific — a direct attempt on the life of the very person the entire community of Medina, including Banu al-Nadir themselves as signatories to the Constitution, had agreed to recognize as their arbiter and protector of the city's peace.

{/* page */}

## Warned in Time

Before this plot could be carried out, the Prophet ﷺ received revelation informing him of exactly what was being planned above him. He rose from his seated position and departed the gathering immediately, offering no explanation to those around him in the moment, and made his way back toward the main area of Medina.

His companions رضي الله عنهم who had accompanied him, noticing his sudden and unexplained departure, followed after him, understandably confused about what had prompted such an abrupt end to what had appeared to be an ordinary discussion. It was only once he had returned to safety that he explained to them what Banu al-Nadir had been planning, and the gravity of what had nearly occurred became clear to everyone present.

## A Direct Breach of the Constitution

It is worth returning explicitly, at this point, to the framework we established in Section 5 regarding the Constitution of Medina, because this incident represents one of the clearest possible tests of whether that document's protections and obligations carried genuine, enforceable weight.

We examined how the Constitution specifically bound the Jewish tribes of Medina, including Banu al-Nadir, to commitments of mutual security — agreeing not to support any enemy against the Muslim community, and specifically agreeing to bring disputes to the Prophet ﷺ for peaceful resolution rather than pursuing unilateral or violent action. A calculated assassination plot, hatched during precisely the kind of peaceful dispute-resolution meeting the Constitution had been designed to facilitate, represented about as direct and unambiguous a violation of that agreement as could be imagined.

## A More Severe Category of Breach

Recall the situation we examined in Unit 16 regarding Banu Qaynuqa — a conflict that originated from a specific marketplace humiliation, escalated through a chain of individual retaliatory actions, and ultimately resulted in the tribe's withdrawal into open defiance of the Constitution's terms. That situation, while genuinely serious, arose from an escalating dispute rather than calculated, premeditated conspiracy targeting the Prophet ﷺ specifically.

The plot of Banu al-Nadir belongs to a different and more severe category entirely. This was not an escalation from an unrelated grievance. It was a direct, specific, and deliberate attempt to kill the very man the entire community, including Banu al-Nadir themselves, had formally agreed to recognize as their common arbiter — undertaken while he sat in good faith attending to exactly the kind of peaceful dispute resolution that recognition was meant to provide.

This distinction in severity matters considerably for understanding the response that followed, which we examine in our next lesson. While the Prophet ﷺ would again demonstrate the same pattern of measured proportionality we observed with Banu Qaynuqa, the heightened seriousness of this particular breach — premeditated assassination rather than an escalating dispute — helps explain why this episode is remembered, even amid the relatively restrained final outcome, as a moment of genuine crisis for the security of the entire Muslim community in Medina.

## What Followed

With the plot against his life now exposed and understood, the Prophet ﷺ faced a clear and urgent question regarding how to respond to a signatory community that had moved from the theoretical possibility of breach into an actual, specific, and nearly successful attempt on his life.

The response he formulated, and Banu al-Nadir's reaction to it, forms the subject of our next lesson, as we examine the expulsion that followed and what it reveals about the continued functioning, even under genuine strain, of the political order established by the Constitution of Medina.
