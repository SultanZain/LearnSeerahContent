## A Moment Twenty Years in the Making

The moment finally arrived that this curriculum has been building toward since we first examined the Hijrah in Section 5 — the Prophet's ﷺ entry into the city of his birth, the city that had persecuted him for thirteen years as we traced in extensive detail across Section 4, and that had forced his exile through an assassination plot examined so carefully in that same section.

{/* page */}

## Organized Entry, Minimal Force

The Prophet ﷺ organized his substantial forces into several distinct columns, entering Mecca from different directions simultaneously, and he gave clear and explicit instructions that fighting should be avoided wherever it could reasonably be prevented, with force reserved only for situations involving direct and active resistance.

This instruction reflected the same deliberate intention to minimize bloodshed that had already been communicated through the specific arrangements made with Abu Sufyan <span className="font-ar">رضي الله عنه</span>, examined in the previous lesson — the safety of his own household, alongside other designated places of refuge throughout the city, established well in advance of the actual entry.

## A Posture of Humility, Not Triumph

Perhaps the most striking and instructive detail preserved from this pivotal moment concerns the Prophet's ﷺ own physical bearing as he entered the city.

Rather than displaying the pride or celebration that might be expected of any conqueror entering a city after decades of persecution and struggle, the Prophet ﷺ rode with his head bowed so low in humility that it reportedly nearly touched the saddle of his camel. He recited verses of the Quran continuously throughout his entry, his attention directed not toward his own achievement or triumph, but toward gratitude and humility before Allah.

This posture reveals something profound about the character this curriculum has traced consistently since our earliest examination of his life in Section 3 — a man for whom even the most significant and long-awaited moment of his entire mission remained an occasion for humility rather than pride, gratitude toward Allah rather than celebration of personal accomplishment.

## Limited Resistance and the Prophet's ﷺ Concern

Despite the general instruction to avoid fighting wherever possible, limited resistance did occur in one section of the city. Khalid ibn al-Walid's <span className="font-ar">رضي الله عنه</span> column encountered a small group who chose to fight rather than accept the peaceful entry, resulting in a brief engagement and casualties on both sides.

Upon learning of this bloodshed, the Prophet ﷺ expressed genuine and visible concern, questioning Khalid <span className="font-ar">رضي الله عنه</span> directly regarding the specific circumstances that had led to this fighting despite the clear general instruction to avoid conflict wherever it could reasonably be prevented. This response reflects, once again, the consistent character we have traced throughout this entire curriculum — genuine concern for minimizing loss of life even in a moment of decisive military and political triumph.

## A Fulfillment of Earlier Prophecy

This remarkably peaceful and largely bloodless conquest, achieved despite the substantial scale of the forces involved and the extended and painful history of conflict this curriculum has traced across this entire section, connects meaningfully back to a specific prophecy we examined in Unit 19.

We recall the moment during the digging of the trench when the Prophet ﷺ struck a particularly difficult rock and described sparks of light emerging with each strike, associating these sparks with future conquests he named specifically. This peaceful entry into Mecca stands as a further and significant demonstration of that broader pattern of fulfilled prophecy, reinforcing the consistent connection this curriculum has traced between the Prophet's ﷺ specific statements throughout his mission and their subsequent, historically documented fulfillment.

## What Remained Ahead

With the city now under Muslim control and achieved with remarkably little bloodshed given the scale and history involved, the most significant and consequential moment of this entire conquest still remained ahead — the Prophet's ﷺ decision regarding how to treat the very people and leadership of Mecca who had persecuted him and his followers for over two decades.

This decision, and the extraordinary act of mercy it would produce, forms the subject of our next lesson — one of the most profound and historically significant demonstrations of forgiveness in the entire history of human civilization.
