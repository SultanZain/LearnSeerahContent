## A Careful Arrangement Before the Storm

Having continued forward with approximately seven hundred men despite the significant withdrawal of Abdullah ibn Ubayy's group examined in the previous lesson, the Prophet ﷺ arrived at the foot of Mount Uhud and arranged his forces with considerable strategic care.

He positioned the army with the mountain itself protecting their rear, eliminating the possibility of Quraysh's considerably larger force surrounding them from that direction — a sound tactical decision given the severe numerical disadvantage the Muslims faced.

## The Archers on the Hill

The most consequential element of this arrangement involved a specific hill that controlled a key approach toward the Muslim position from behind. The Prophet ﷺ stationed approximately fifty archers there, under the command of Abdullah ibn Jubayr <span className="font-ar">رضي الله عنه</span>, with an instruction that the narrations preserve with notable precision and repetition:

> *"Protect our rear. If you see us being killed, do not come to help us, and if you see us collecting the spoils, do not join us."*

This instruction was explicit, repeated, and left no reasonable ambiguity about its meaning: regardless of how the battle appeared to be unfolding — whether the Muslims seemed to be winning decisively or suffering significant losses — the archers were to hold their assigned position until they received direct command otherwise.

## The Engagement Opens

With positions arranged, the battle began. Despite facing an army of roughly three thousand with a force of only around seven hundred, the Muslim soldiers fought with a discipline and ferocity that produced immediate and significant results.

Hamzah ibn Abd al-Muttalib <span className="font-ar">رضي الله عنه</span>, whose remarkable transformation from tribal honor to genuine faith we examined in detail earlier in this section, fought with the kind of fearless intensity that had defined his reputation since long before his acceptance of Islam. He and other companions رضي الله عنهم pressed forward with such effectiveness that Quraysh's forward lines began to crack under the pressure.

What followed was a genuine and dramatic shift in the battle's momentum. Quraysh's soldiers, facing a smaller but ferociously disciplined opponent, began to break formation and retreat from the field, abandoning equipment and supplies in their hasty withdrawal.

## A Battlefield That Looked Won

For those Muslim soldiers fighting on the ground, witnessing Quraysh's lines collapse and their enemies fleeing the battlefield, the natural and entirely understandable conclusion was that the battle had been decisively won — a second consecutive triumph against overwhelming numerical odds, echoing the victory at Badr that we studied in such detail in the previous unit.

This perception, while premature, was not unreasonable given what was visibly occurring on the field. Quraysh's retreat was genuine, not a calculated tactical feint. The spoils of war — equipment, supplies, and possessions abandoned by the fleeing soldiers — lay visible and available across the battlefield.

It was this perception of decisive victory that began drawing soldiers away from their assigned positions, including, most critically, a significant portion of the archers stationed on the hill under Abdullah ibn Jubayr's <span className="font-ar">رضي الله عنه</span> command — setting in motion the events that would transform this developing triumph into one of the most difficult days the early Muslim community would face.

## Why This Phase Matters

It is essential to hold this early phase of the battle clearly in mind as we proceed to examine what happened next, because it establishes something crucial for understanding the lessons Uhud is meant to teach.

The difficulties that followed at Uhud did not result from any deficiency in the courage of the Muslim soldiers — their fighting in this opening phase was genuinely exceptional against severe numerical odds. They did not result from any flaw in the Prophet's ﷺ strategic arrangement — his positioning of the archers on the hill was precisely the kind of foresight that, had the instruction been followed completely, would likely have secured a clean and complete victory. And they did not result from any withdrawal of Allah's support, as we will see reaffirmed even amid the difficulties ahead.

What follows resulted from a specific and identifiable departure from a clear instruction at a precise moment — a distinction that shapes the entire theological and practical lesson that the Quran draws from this day, which we will examine carefully across the lessons ahead.

## The Moment That Changed Everything

As more and more of the archers, believing the battle decisively won, began descending from their position to join in collecting the abandoned spoils, the hill that had been protecting the Muslim rear was left dangerously undefended.

This was the opening that the remaining Quraysh cavalry, under the command of Khalid ibn al-Walid — a formidable commander who had not yet accepted Islam and would, years later, become one of the most celebrated generals in Islamic history — had been watching for, and would now exploit with devastating effect.

What happened next, examined fully in our next lesson, transformed what had appeared to be a second consecutive decisive Muslim victory into one of the most painful and instructive days in the entire history of the early Muslim community.
