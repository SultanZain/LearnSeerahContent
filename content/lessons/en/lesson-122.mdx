## A Transformed Community Marches Forward

Following Quraysh's clear and demonstrable breach of the Treaty of Hudaybiyyah examined in the previous unit, the Prophet ﷺ organized an expedition of a scale that stands as remarkable testament to the transformation the Muslim community had undergone across the entire span of the Medinan period this curriculum has traced in such detail.

{/* page */}

## From Hundreds to Ten Thousand

The force that gathered for this march numbered approximately ten thousand men — a scale worth pausing to appreciate fully against the backdrop of everything we have examined throughout this section. This was a community that, at Badr in Unit 15, had fielded barely over three hundred men against Quraysh's army of roughly a thousand. At Uhud in Unit 17, following Abdullah ibn Ubayy's damaging withdrawal, the remaining Muslim force had numbered only around seven hundred against Quraysh's three thousand.

Now, roughly eight years after the Hijrah, ten thousand men answered the call to march toward Mecca — a transformation reflecting not merely growth in raw numbers, but the accumulated strength, organization, and widening alliance network the Muslim community had built through every crisis and every period of peace examined across this entire section.

## Deliberate and Careful Secrecy

The Prophet ﷺ took genuine and deliberate care to maintain secrecy regarding this expedition's true destination and purpose, a measure of strategic caution consistent with the careful planning we have traced throughout nearly every significant military undertaking examined in this section.

Many of his own companions رضي الله عنهم did not learn the expedition's actual destination until the march was already well underway. This deliberate secrecy served an important strategic purpose: preventing Quraysh from receiving sufficient advance warning that might have allowed them to organize a coordinated defense, seek external alliances, or mount some form of preemptive response before the substantial Muslim force could actually arrive at the city's outskirts.

## Connecting to the Original Vision

This march connects meaningfully back to the dream we examined in considerable detail at the beginning of Unit 23 — the Prophet's ﷺ vision of himself entering Mecca peacefully to perform the rites of Umrah, a vision that had originally prompted the journey to Hudaybiyyah years earlier.

That original vision had already found significant, if partial, fulfillment through the Umrah al-Qada performed the following year, during which the Prophet's ﷺ marriage to Maymunah <span className="font-ar">رضي الله عنها</span>, examined in detail in Unit 21, had also taken place. This new march toward Mecca now represented something even fuller and more complete — not merely a temporary pilgrimage visit under negotiated terms, but the possibility of a genuine and lasting resolution to the entire extended history of conflict between the Muslim community and the city of the Prophet's ﷺ own birth.

## What This Journey Meant to the Muhajirin

For the muhajirin specifically, this march carried an emotional and historical weight that extends across the entire arc of this curriculum's examination of their story. We traced their painful and forced departure from Mecca during the persecution examined extensively throughout Section 4 — the mockery, the boycott, the physical torture, and ultimately the assassination plot that forced the Hijrah itself, examined in such detail in Section 5.

Now, these same individuals, or in some cases their children who had grown up hearing these stories, approached the city of their birth not as vulnerable refugees fleeing under threat of death, but as part of a substantial and organized force of ten thousand, arriving under circumstances of genuine strength and security. The emotional significance of this transformation — from forced exile to confident return — cannot be overstated for those who had lived through, or grown up within the living memory of, the events this curriculum has traced since its earliest sections.

## What Awaited at Mecca

As the substantial Muslim force approached the outskirts of Mecca, word of their arrival inevitably reached the city's leadership, creating a moment of genuine crisis and uncertainty for Quraysh — a crisis that would produce one of the most significant individual conversations in the entire history of early Islam, involving Abu Sufyan ibn Harb himself, examined directly in our next lesson.
