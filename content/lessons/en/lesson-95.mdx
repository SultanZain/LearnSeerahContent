## Continuing the Household

Following the marriages to Sawdah and Aisha <span className="font-ar">رضي الله عنهما</span> examined in our previous lesson, the Prophet ﷺ entered into three additional marriages during this period of the Medinan years — each carrying its own specific circumstances that connect directly to the broader patterns we established at the beginning of this unit.

## Hafsah bint Umar رضي الله عنها

Hafsah <span className="font-ar">رضي الله عنها</span> was the daughter of Umar ibn al-Khattab <span className="font-ar">رضي الله عنه</span> — the companion whose dramatic conversion after setting out to kill the Prophet ﷺ we examined in detail in Section 4, and whose formidable directness and zeal for justice we have seen demonstrated repeatedly throughout this curriculum.

She had been married to Khunays ibn Hudhafah <span className="font-ar">رضي الله عنه</span>, a companion who fought at the Battle of Badr that we studied extensively earlier in this section. Khunays <span className="font-ar">رضي الله عنه</span> died from wounds sustained in that battle, leaving Hafsah <span className="font-ar">رضي الله عنها</span> a young widow.

Umar <span className="font-ar">رضي الله عنه</span>, concerned for his daughter's future, approached both Abu Bakr <span className="font-ar">رضي الله عنه</span> and Uthman <span className="font-ar">رضي الله عنه</span> regarding marriage, and when neither arrangement proceeded, the Prophet ﷺ himself proposed marriage to her. This marriage exemplifies precisely the pattern of care for war widows we identified earlier in this unit, while also further strengthening the already profound bond between the Prophet ﷺ and Umar <span className="font-ar">رضي الله عنه</span>, one of the most significant figures in the entire history of the early Muslim community.

## Zaynab bint Khuzaymah رضي الله عنها — Mother of the Poor

Zaynab bint Khuzaymah <span className="font-ar">رضي الله عنها</span> carried, even before her marriage to the Prophet ﷺ, a reputation within the Medinan community that had already earned her a lasting title: Umm al-Masakin, the Mother of the Poor.

This title reflected genuine and well-established conduct — a consistent and generous care for the needy of Medina that had become so central to her known character that the title attached permanently to her name, used by the community as a natural way of identifying her long before any connection to marriage with the Prophet ﷺ.

Her marriage to the Prophet ﷺ represented, in this sense, less a transformation of her character than a natural continuation of values she had already demonstrated consistently. Sadly, she did not live long after this marriage, passing away only a few months later — but her memory and the title she earned through her own conduct remain a lasting part of the historical record of this period.

## Umm Salamah رضي الله عنها — A Woman of Wisdom

Umm Salamah <span className="font-ar">رضي الله عنها</span>, whose birth name was Hind bint Abi Umayyah, had been among the earliest emigrants to Abyssinia during the persecution we examined extensively in Section 4, and later among those who made the Hijrah to Medina. She had been married to Abu Salamah <span className="font-ar">رضي الله عنه</span>, a companion who was wounded at Uhud — the battle we examined in such detail earlier in this section — and who later died from complications related to that wound.

Left a widow with young children in circumstances of genuine difficulty, her marriage to the Prophet ﷺ provided the protection and stability that her situation required. But Umm Salamah's <span className="font-ar">رضي الله عنها</span> significance within the early Muslim community extended considerably beyond this initial circumstance.

As we will examine directly when we reach the unit on the Treaty of Hudaybiyyah later in this section, Umm Salamah <span className="font-ar">رضي الله عنها</span> provided the Prophet ﷺ with wise and decisive counsel during a moment of considerable tension with his companions رضي الله عنهم regarding the terms of that treaty — counsel that proved instrumental in resolving a genuinely delicate situation. This episode, which we will examine in full when we reach it, demonstrates clearly that her role within the household extended to genuine influence and trusted wisdom on matters of significant consequence for the entire community.

## What Connects These Three Marriages

Considered together, these three marriages reinforce and illustrate the broader framework we established at the beginning of this unit regarding the genuine range of purposes behind the Prophet's ﷺ marriages during this period.

Hafsah's <span className="font-ar">رضي الله عنها</span> marriage reflected both care for a war widow and connection to Umar <span className="font-ar">رضي الله عنه</span>, one of the central figures of the entire early Muslim community. Zaynab bint Khuzaymah's <span className="font-ar">رضي الله عنها</span> marriage reflected continuity with an already-established character of extraordinary compassion. Umm Salamah's <span className="font-ar">رضي الله عنها</span> marriage brought into the Prophet's ﷺ household a woman whose wisdom would prove genuinely consequential for the broader community, not merely for the household itself.
