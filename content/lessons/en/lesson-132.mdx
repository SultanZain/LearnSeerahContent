## Not Everyone Answered the Call

We examined in the previous lesson the remarkable response of tens of thousands of Muslims who answered the difficult call to Tabuk despite genuine and significant hardship. This lesson turns to those who did not — and the important distinctions the Quran itself draws between different categories of absence.

{/* page */}

## Hypocrites and Sincere Believers Distinguished

The Quran addresses this matter with careful and important distinction, connecting directly back to the pattern of hypocrisy this curriculum examined in detail throughout Unit 16. Those whose faith was insincere offered fabricated excuses for their absence, seeking to avoid both the difficulty of the expedition and any social or spiritual consequence for their failure to participate.

<Quran surah="9" ayah="94" />

*"They will make excuses to you when you have returned to them. Say, 'Make no excuse — never will we believe you.'"*

This passage reflects the same pattern of insincerity this curriculum has traced consistently since our examination of Abdullah ibn Ubayy and the broader phenomenon of hypocrisy in Unit 16 — outward profession accompanied by a fundamental absence of genuine internal commitment.

## Three Sincere Men Who Chose Honesty

Amid this broader group of insincere excuse-makers, a small number of genuinely sincere believers had also failed to join the expedition, for reasons that reflected genuine human weakness and procrastination rather than any deliberate hostility toward the Muslim community or its mission.

Ka'b ibn Malik, Hilal ibn Umayyah, and Murarah ibn al-Rabi <span className="font-ar">رضي الله عنهم</span> were specifically identified among this group. Unlike the hypocrites, these three men had no legitimate excuse for their absence — but rather than fabricating one, they chose, when questioned directly by the Prophet ﷺ, to tell the truth honestly and completely about their failure.

{/* page */}

## A Punishment Harder Than Battle

The Prophet ﷺ, in response to this honest admission, instructed the entire Muslim community to withdraw from speaking with these three men entirely, for a period of fifty days — a complete social isolation within their own community, among people who had known and loved them throughout years of shared struggle and sacrifice.

Ka'b ibn Malik <span className="font-ar">رضي الله عنه</span> later described this period with genuine and moving detail, explaining that even his own close relatives would not speak with him during this time, and that the earth itself, despite its vastness, felt constrained and narrow to him — language that echoes directly the description we examined regarding Hunayn in an earlier lesson of this unit, where the Quran used similar imagery to describe the disorientation of genuine crisis. Ka'b ibn Malik <span className="font-ar">رضي الله عنه</span> stated plainly that this period of isolation proved more genuinely difficult to bear than any physical hardship or punishment could have been.

## A Joyful Resolution

After fifty days of this genuine and difficult isolation, resolution came through direct Quranic revelation, announcing explicitly Allah's acceptance of the repentance of these three sincere men:

<Quran surah="9" ayah="118" />

*"And to the three who were left behind, until when the earth had constrained them despite its vastness and their souls had confined them and they were certain that there is no refuge from Allah except in Him — then He turned to them so they could repent. Indeed, Allah is the Accepting of Repentance, the Merciful."*

Ka'b ibn Malik <span className="font-ar">رضي الله عنه</span> described the overwhelming joy of this moment, running to the mosque upon hearing the news, his heart filled with a gratitude so profound that he immediately resolved to give away his wealth in thanksgiving for this mercy.

## The Enduring Value of Complete Honesty

This episode teaches a lesson of profound and lasting significance regarding the value the Quran places on complete and sincere honesty, even when that honesty carries genuine and difficult consequence.

The hypocrites who lied avoided the immediate social difficulty that Ka'b ibn Malik <span className="font-ar">رضي الله عنه</span> and his two companions رضي الله عنهم endured — but they received no comparable divine acknowledgment or celebrated forgiveness. The three sincere men who told the truth suffered genuinely and significantly for fifty days, yet their honesty ultimately earned them a specific, permanent, and celebrated place within the Quran itself, their story preserved and recited by Muslims across every subsequent generation as a model of sincere repentance rewarded.

## What Remained Ahead

With this moving episode now examined in full, we turn in our final lesson of this unit to a broader and remarkable development that followed the Tabuk expedition and the consolidation of Muslim strength across Arabia — the Year of Delegations, during which tribes from across the entire peninsula came to Medina to embrace Islam, marking the effective completion of the Prophet's ﷺ mission to unify Arabia under the message of Tawhid.
