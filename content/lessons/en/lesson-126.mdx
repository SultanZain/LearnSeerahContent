## Restoring a House to Its Purpose

With the general amnesty examined in the previous lesson now established, the Prophet ﷺ turned his attention to a task of profound symbolic and spiritual significance — the physical cleansing of the Kaaba itself, addressing directly the centuries of accumulated idolatry that had transformed this sacred house from a center of pure Tawhid into precisely the kind of religious confusion this curriculum examined in such detail in Section 4.

{/* page */}

## Three Hundred and Sixty Idols

We recall, from our examination in Section 4 of the general state of pre-Islamic Arabian religious practice, that the Kaaba had come to house approximately three hundred and sixty idols by the time of the Prophet's ﷺ birth — a staggering accumulation representing centuries of gradual departure from the pure worship of Allah alone that Ibrahim <span className="font-ar">عليه السلام</span> and Ismail <span className="font-ar">عليه السلام</span> had originally established when constructing this house, a story we traced with such care across Section 1 and Section 2 of this entire curriculum.

This same physical reality confronted the Prophet ﷺ now, at the culmination of his mission, as he stood within the very structure his own ancestors had built specifically and exclusively for the worship of the One God.

## Truth Has Come, Falsehood Has Departed

The Prophet ﷺ proceeded through the Kaaba and its surrounding area, pointing at each idol with his staff, and as each one was subsequently removed and destroyed, he recited a specific and theologically powerful verse:

<Quran surah="17" ayah="81" />

*"Say, truth has come, and falsehood has departed. Indeed, falsehood is bound to depart."*

This recitation connected the physical act of removal directly to the deeper theological reality this moment represented. This was never simply about clearing away religious clutter — it was the restoration of genuine truth to a place that had been overtaken by falsehood for centuries, exactly as the verse itself declares falsehood's inherent and inevitable impermanence.

## Fulfilling a Dua Made Centuries Earlier

This moment carries a theological weight that connects directly and powerfully back to something we examined with particular care in Section 1 of this curriculum — the dua that Ibrahim <span className="font-ar">عليه السلام</span> and Ismail <span className="font-ar">عليه السلام</span> made together as they raised the very walls of this sacred structure:

<Quran surah="2" ayah="129" />

*"Our Lord, and send among them a messenger from themselves who will recite to them Your verses and teach them the Book and wisdom and purify them."*

This cleansing represents the complete and visible fulfillment of that ancient dua, made by two Prophets we studied extensively in Section 1, generations before Muhammad ﷺ was even born. The Prophet ﷺ standing within the Kaaba, removing the idols that had accumulated over centuries and restoring this house to its original and singular purpose, embodied the direct and literal answer to a prayer offered by his own ancestors long before this moment arrived.

## Correcting Even Subtle Distortions

The care and thoroughness of this cleansing extended beyond the obvious removal of physical idols. According to the narrations, an image was discovered within the Kaaba depicting Ibrahim <span className="font-ar">عليه السلام</span> in a manner suggesting he had engaged in a form of divination — a practice entirely inconsistent with everything we examined regarding his character and conduct in Section 1.

The Prophet ﷺ expressed clear displeasure at this depiction, correcting the record explicitly by noting that Ibrahim <span className="font-ar">عليه السلام</span> had never engaged in any such practice. This detail reveals something significant about the comprehensive character of this cleansing — it extended not merely to the obvious idols themselves, but to subtler distortions that had crept into how even the Prophets of the past, examined so carefully throughout this curriculum, were being remembered and represented within this sacred space.

## A House Restored

With this cleansing complete, the Kaaba stood, for the first time in centuries, restored entirely to the singular purpose Ibrahim <span className="font-ar">عليه السلام</span> and Ismail <span className="font-ar">عليه السلام</span> had originally established: a house dedicated exclusively to the worship of Allah alone, free from the accumulated idolatry that had defined its condition for so many generations.

This moment stands as one of the most significant culminations of the entire theological arc this curriculum has traced since its opening lessons in Section 1 — the restoration of pure Tawhid to the very physical center of Arabian religious life, achieved through the final Prophet ﷺ whose arrival Ibrahim <span className="font-ar">عليه السلام</span> and Ismail <span className="font-ar">عليه السلام</span> had specifically prayed for centuries earlier.

We turn in our final lesson of this unit to one more deeply symbolic moment that occurred in the aftermath of this conquest — the sound of the adhan called from atop the Kaaba itself by Bilal <span className="font-ar">رضي الله عنه</span>, the very man whose torture on the burning sands of Mecca we examined in such detail in Section 4.
