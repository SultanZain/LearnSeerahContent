## Returning to a Place of Painful Memory

Following the significant victory at Hunayn and the resolution regarding the distribution of spoils examined in the previous lessons of this unit, the Muslim forces proceeded toward a location that carried genuine and painful personal significance for the Prophet ﷺ — the city of Ta'if.

{/* page */}

## Why the Muslim Forces Proceeded to Ta'if

A significant portion of the defeated forces of Thaqif, following their loss at Hunayn, withdrew and fortified themselves within the walls of Ta'if — the very city whose leadership and populace we examined in such detail in Section 4, when we traced the Prophet's ﷺ desperate journey there seeking support after the Year of Grief, and the devastating rejection and physical stoning that followed, driving him from the city with bleeding feet.

This remaining pocket of military resistance required the Muslim forces to pursue and address the situation, leading to a siege of the fortified city.

{/* page */}

{/* quiz: q1 */}

{/* page */}

## A Difficult Siege and a Deliberate Withdrawal

The siege of Ta'if proved genuinely challenging given the city's strong fortifications, which had been constructed specifically to withstand exactly this kind of sustained military pressure. After a period of concerted but ultimately unsuccessful attempts to breach these defenses, the Prophet ﷺ made a deliberate and considered decision: rather than continuing the siege indefinitely at potentially significant further cost, he chose to withdraw his forces.

This decision reflected a broader wisdom and trust — that the people of Ta'if would, in time, come to embrace Islam through means other than forced military conquest, consistent with a hope the Prophet ﷺ had expressed regarding this very city years earlier under circumstances of far greater personal pain and vulnerability.

{/* page */}

{/* page */}

## The Fulfillment of an Earlier Hope

We recall, from our detailed examination in Section 4, the moment when the Angel of the Mountains came to the Prophet ﷺ in the garden outside Ta'if, offering to collapse the two mountains flanking the city onto its population as punishment for the stoning and rejection he had just endured. His response, at that moment of profound personal suffering and vulnerability, was remarkable: *"No — rather I hope that Allah will bring from their descendants people who worship Allah alone and associate nothing with Him."*

Within a relatively short period following this withdrawal from the siege, a delegation from Ta'if came to the Prophet ﷺ voluntarily, embracing Islam without the need for further military conflict or forced conquest. This outcome represents the direct, moving, and historically documented fulfillment of the very hope the Prophet ﷺ had expressed at his moment of greatest personal pain regarding this specific city, years before this later military campaign had even become necessary.

## The Long Arc of Patience and Mercy

This complete story, spanning from the devastating rejection examined in Section 4 through this eventual and voluntary embrace of Islam, offers one of the most powerful demonstrations throughout this entire curriculum of the long-term wisdom embedded in choosing mercy and patience over immediate retribution, even when that retribution might have seemed, in the moment, entirely justified.

The Prophet ﷺ, bleeding and rejected at his lowest point in that garden outside Ta'if, chose hope over vengeance when offered the power to destroy the very city that had just wronged him so severely. Years later, that same choice of hope produced exactly the outcome it had anticipated — not through the destruction the Angel of the Mountains had offered, but through the voluntary and sincere conversion of the descendants of the very people who had once driven him from their gates with stones.
