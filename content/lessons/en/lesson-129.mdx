## A Question of Wise and Sensitive Distribution

Following the victory at Hunayn examined in the previous lesson, the Muslim forces captured substantial spoils of war — livestock, goods, and other material wealth belonging to the defeated tribes of Hawazin and Thaqif. The question of how to distribute these spoils required careful and considered wisdom, given the diverse composition of the Muslim community by this point in the Medinan period.

{/* page */}

## Winning Hearts Still Being Won

The Prophet ﷺ directed particularly generous portions of this substantial wealth toward recent converts from Mecca and the surrounding tribes — individuals whose acceptance of Islam remained relatively new, following the conquest examined in the previous unit, and whose continued and deepening commitment to the faith benefited from this demonstration of the Prophet's ﷺ genuine generosity and care.

This category of distribution connects to a principle the Quran itself establishes explicitly regarding the recipients of Zakah, which we examined in detail earlier in this section — specifically the category of *mu'allafatu qulubuhum*, those whose hearts are to be reconciled or won over, reflecting a wise and deliberate approach to strengthening the still-developing faith of those who had only recently embraced Islam.

## A Reasonable Concern Among the Ansar

Given this distribution pattern, some among the Ansar — whose extraordinary history of sacrifice this curriculum has traced since the pledges of Aqabah examined in Section 5, through the Brotherhood that embedded the muhajirin within their own families and resources, through the fighting at Badr, Uhud, and the Trench that has occupied nearly every unit of this entire section — expressed genuine concern and even disappointment.

Their reasoning was understandable on its own terms: they had proven their loyalty and commitment to the Muslim community repeatedly, across years of genuine hardship and sacrifice, while these recent converts had only just embraced the faith following the conquest of Mecca. Why, then, should such generous portions of these spoils be directed toward the newer converts while the long-proven Ansar received comparatively less?

## A Question That Reframed Everything

When this concern reached the Prophet ﷺ, he gathered the Ansar and addressed them directly, with evident emotion and personal engagement rather than distant or dismissive correction.

He asked them: *"O assembly of the Ansar, would you not be content that other people should go away with sheep and camels while you go away with the Messenger of Allah to your own homes?"*

This question reframed the entire matter completely. The material wealth being distributed to the recent converts was, in the fullest sense, a temporary and worldly consideration — livestock and goods that would be consumed or traded away in time. What the Ansar possessed instead was something of infinitely greater and more enduring value: the Prophet ﷺ himself, choosing to remain and dwell among them in Medina, the very city that bore their name and their sacrifice.

## A Moment of Genuine Emotion

The narrations describe the Ansar responding to this question with tears, moved deeply by the way the Prophet ﷺ had reframed their situation and reaffirmed the profound bond between them. They declared their complete satisfaction, affirming that indeed, they would choose the Prophet's ﷺ presence among them over any material wealth that could possibly be distributed.

This moment of resolution reflects a mutual and deeply felt love between the Prophet ﷺ and the community that had received him when his own city had cast him out — a love that material distribution, however generous or carefully considered, could never adequately measure or replace.

## A Continuation of Established Character

This episode reinforces directly and powerfully the same remarkable character of the Ansar that this curriculum examined in such detail in Section 5, when studying the pledges of Aqabah and the extraordinary spirit of ithaar — preferring others over oneself — that the Quran itself praised in describing their treatment of the muhajirin upon their arrival in Medina.

Years had passed since those earlier demonstrations of sacrifice, and the Muslim community had grown and changed considerably across the events this entire section has traced. Yet the fundamental character of the Ansar, when tested once again through this specific moment of apparent slight, proved to be exactly what it had always been — a genuine preference for what truly mattered, the presence and love of the Prophet ﷺ himself, over any consideration of material wealth or worldly recognition.

## What Remains Ahead

With this moment of reconciliation and reaffirmed loyalty now examined, we turn in our final lesson of this unit to a matter of unfinished business connecting directly back to one of the most emotionally significant episodes this curriculum examined in Section 4 — a return, in changed circumstances, to the city of Ta'if.
