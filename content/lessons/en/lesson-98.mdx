## A Return to the City of His Birth

The final marriage of the Prophet's ﷺ life occurred under circumstances carrying particular emotional and historical weight — during the Umrah al-Qada, the makeup pilgrimage performed the year following the Treaty of Hudaybiyyah, a treaty and its remarkable aftermath that we will examine in full detail in the unit ahead.

This context matters considerably for understanding the significance of this marriage. For the first time in years, the Prophet ﷺ was entering Mecca — the city of his birth, the city that had persecuted him for thirteen years as we examined extensively in Section 4, the city he had been forced to flee during the Hijrah we studied in Section 5 — not as a refugee or under threat, but peacefully, as part of an agreed and honored pilgrimage.

## Maymunah bint al-Harith رضي الله عنها

It was during this significant and emotionally weighted visit that the Prophet ﷺ married Maymunah bint al-Harith <span className="font-ar">رضي الله عنها</span>. The marriage itself was arranged through the Prophet's ﷺ uncle Abbas <span className="font-ar">رضي الله عنه</span>, whom we have encountered repeatedly throughout this curriculum — presiding over the second pledge of Aqabah before his own conversion, sending word of Quraysh's mobilization before Uhud, and now facilitating this final and significant marriage.

## A Household Connected to the Future of Islamic Scholarship

Maymunah's <span className="font-ar">رضي الله عنها</span> family connections carried particular significance for the future development of Islamic scholarship and history. She was the maternal aunt of Abdullah ibn Abbas <span className="font-ar">رضي الله عنه</span> — a young companion رضي الله عنه whose contributions to the field of Quranic interpretation would become so foundational and so extensive that he is remembered across Islamic scholarly tradition as one of the greatest interpreters of the Quran the Muslim community has ever produced.

Her family connections also extended to Khalid ibn al-Walid <span className="font-ar">رضي الله عنه</span> — the very commander whose tactical skill at Uhud we examined in detail earlier in this section, leading the cavalry maneuver that exploited the archers' departure from their position. Following his own eventual conversion to Islam, Khalid <span className="font-ar">رضي الله عنه</span> would go on to become one of the most celebrated and effective military commanders in the entire history of Islam, a transformation from opponent to champion that we will touch upon again as we continue through the remaining units of this section.

## Closing This Chapter With Hope

This final marriage, occurring at a moment of peaceful return to the city that had once cast the Prophet ﷺ out under threat of death, fittingly closes this chapter of his marital life on a note of hope and reconciliation.

Considered across the full arc of this entire unit, the pattern we have traced carries a genuine coherence. The earliest marriages following Khadijah's <span className="font-ar">رضي الله عنها</span> death, to Sawdah and Aisha <span className="font-ar">رضي الله عنهما</span>, reflected the immediate needs of rebuilding a household and deepening bonds with the Prophet's ﷺ closest companion. The subsequent marriages to Hafsah, Zaynab bint Khuzaymah, and Umm Salamah <span className="font-ar">رضي الله عنهن</span> reflected the ongoing care for war widows and the strengthening of connections to key companions رضي الله عنهم. The marriage to Zaynab bint Jahsh <span className="font-ar">رضي الله عنها</span> corrected a mistaken pre-Islamic legal custom through direct divine command. The marriages to Juwayriyyah, Umm Habibah, and Safiyyah <span className="font-ar">رضي الله عنهن</span> transformed relationships of significant conflict into genuine family bonds. And now, this final marriage to Maymunah <span className="font-ar">رضي الله عنها</span> occurs precisely at the moment the Prophet ﷺ returns peacefully to the city of his own birth.
