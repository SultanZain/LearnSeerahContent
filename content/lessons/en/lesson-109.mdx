## Terms That Appeared Deeply Unfavorable

Following the tension examined in the previous lesson, negotiations between the Prophet ﷺ and Quraysh's representative, Suhayl ibn Amr, produced a treaty whose specific terms, considered at face value, appeared to favor Quraysh considerably.

The Muslims agreed to return to Medina that year without performing the Umrah they had traveled specifically to complete, with the promise of returning the following year instead. A ten-year truce was established between the two parties. A specifically asymmetric provision stated that any person from Quraysh who joined the Muslim community without their guardian's permission would be returned to Mecca, while any Muslim who chose to join Quraysh would not be returned to Medina under the same terms. And in a detail that struck many present as particularly difficult, the Prophet ﷺ agreed, at Quraysh's insistence, to remove the phrase "Messenger of Allah" from the written document itself, having the scribe write simply "Muhammad ibn Abdullah" instead.

## Umar's رضي الله عنه Honest Distress

The genuine difficulty these terms presented, even to the most sincere and senior of the Prophet's ﷺ companions رضي الله عنهم, is preserved honestly in the historical record without any attempt to smooth over or minimize this moment of real struggle.

Umar ibn al-Khattab <span className="font-ar">رضي الله عنه</span>, whose directness and zeal for justice we have examined consistently throughout this curriculum, approached the Prophet ﷺ with visible and genuine distress, asking pointed questions: *"Are you not truly the Messenger of Allah? Are we not truly Muslims, and are they not truly polytheists? Then why should we accept humiliation in our religion?"*

The Prophet ﷺ responded with calm and patient assurance: *"I am the Messenger of Allah, and I will not disobey Him, and He will not abandon me."*

This exchange, preserved without embarrassment, models precisely the kind of historical honesty this curriculum has maintained throughout every difficult episode examined across this entire section. Even Umar <span className="font-ar">رضي الله عنه</span>, a man of extraordinary faith and conviction, struggled genuinely in this specific moment to reconcile what appeared before him with his understanding of what victory for the Muslim cause should look like.

## A Moment of Tension Resolved by Wisdom

Following the treaty's completion, the Prophet ﷺ instructed his companions رضي الله عنهم to end their state of pilgrim consecration by shaving their heads and sacrificing their animals, even though they had not been permitted to complete the Umrah itself. Many of the companions رضي الله عنهم, disheartened by the treaty's terms and the disappointment of having come so close to Mecca without completing their intended pilgrimage, hesitated to follow this instruction.

It was at this moment that Umm Salamah <span className="font-ar">رضي الله عنها</span>, whose position within the household and reputation for wisdom we examined in Unit 21, offered counsel that proved immediately decisive. She suggested that the Prophet ﷺ simply perform these actions himself, without repeating the verbal instruction, reasoning that the companions رضي الله عنهم, once they witnessed his own example, would follow without further need for command.

The Prophet ﷺ followed this advice exactly. He shaved his own head and sacrificed his own animal, and upon seeing this, the companions رضي الله عنهم immediately followed his example, resolving the moment of tension completely.

## The Quran's Remarkable Declaration

Shortly after the treaty was concluded, Allah revealed a passage that reframed this entire episode in terms that must have startled many who had witnessed the apparent difficulty of the negotiations:

<Quran surah="48" ayah="1" />

*Indeed, We have given you, O Muhammad, a clear conquest.*

A clear conquest — not a defeat, not a difficult compromise merely tolerated, but a genuine and explicitly declared victory, delivered through direct revelation regarding an agreement that had appeared, by every conventional measure available to those present, to favor their opponents considerably.

## Vindication Within Two Years

The wisdom behind this declaration became increasingly clear as the following two years unfolded. The ten-year truce, whatever its unfavorable appearance at the moment of signing, created something the Muslim community had not experienced since the beginning of the Prophet's ﷺ mission decades earlier: genuine and sustained peace with Quraysh, free from the immediate and constant threat of military confrontation that had defined nearly every year we have examined throughout this entire section, from Badr through Uhud through the Trench.

This peace allowed the Prophet ﷺ to direct his full attention toward dawah, toward the letters to foreign kings and rulers that we will examine in the unit ahead, and toward the general strengthening and expansion of the Muslim community without the constant distraction of military necessity. The historical record demonstrates the result with striking clarity: more people accepted Islam in the two years following the Treaty of Hudaybiyyah than in the entire period preceding it, from the very beginning of the Prophet's ﷺ mission in the cave of Hira through every battle and crisis we have examined across this entire curriculum.

The apparent asymmetries of the treaty's terms, including the very provision Umar <span className="font-ar">رضي الله عنه</span> had found so troubling, ultimately worked in ways that favored the Muslim community's genuine long-term interests. And when Quraysh eventually and decisively broke the treaty themselves — an episode we will examine in a later unit of this section — the Prophet ﷺ found himself with a Muslim community grown so considerably in strength and numbers during this two-year peace that the resulting conquest of Mecca occurred with remarkably little resistance or bloodshed.

## A Lesson in Trusting Allah's Wisdom Beyond Appearances

The Treaty of Hudaybiyyah offers a lesson of genuine and lasting significance, connecting directly to patterns we have traced consistently throughout this curriculum — from the lessons of Uhud examined earlier in this section, to the broader teaching regarding trials and tests we established when studying the Prophets in Section 1.

What appears, in the immediate moment, to be an unfavorable or even humiliating circumstance may, within Allah's fuller wisdom and timing, constitute the very foundation for a genuine and lasting triumph that could not have been achieved through any other path. Umar's <span className="font-ar">رضي الله عنه</span> honest struggle to understand this in the moment does not diminish his faith — it reflects the genuine difficulty of trusting divine wisdom when circumstances appear, by every conventional measure available in that moment, to point in the opposite direction.
