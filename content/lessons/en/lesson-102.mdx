## An Innocent Circumstance

Following one of the Prophet's ﷺ military expeditions during this period of the Medinan years, the returning army made a stop along the journey home. Aisha <span className="font-ar">رضي الله عنها</span>, who had accompanied the expedition as was customary for the Prophet's ﷺ wives during certain campaigns, left the camp briefly during this stop to search for a necklace she had lost.

While she was away searching, the army began its onward march. Because Aisha <span className="font-ar">رضي الله عنها</span> was of slight build, those responsible for her enclosed litter did not notice any difference in its weight when they lifted it onto her camel, and they proceeded under the entirely reasonable assumption that she remained inside as usual.

## Left Behind

When Aisha <span className="font-ar">رضي الله عنها</span> returned from her search for the necklace, she discovered that the entire army had already departed without her. Rather than panicking, she made the sensible decision to remain where she was, reasoning that once her absence was noticed, someone would be sent back to find her.

She wrapped herself in her outer garment and sat down to wait, eventually falling asleep in this position.

## Safwan ibn al-Mu'attal's رضي الله عنه Proper Conduct

It was standard practice for military expeditions of this era to have a designated individual follow some distance behind the main army, specifically tasked with recovering any items, equipment, or people who might have been inadvertently left behind. On this particular expedition, this responsibility fell to Safwan ibn al-Mu'attal <span className="font-ar">رضي الله عنه</span>.

Safwan <span className="font-ar">رضي الله عنه</span> came upon Aisha <span className="font-ar">رضي الله عنها</span> in this circumstance, and recognizing her — he had seen her before the revelation regarding hijab that we examined in the previous unit had been established — he immediately and audibly expressed a statement of recognition and concern for her welfare, without engaging in any private conversation with her.

He brought his camel for her to ride, then walked ahead leading the animal by its reins, guiding her back toward the main army. At no point during this entire episode is there any documented or credible suggestion of impropriety in his conduct. He acted precisely as his assigned duty required, with complete and consistent propriety throughout.

## A Rumor Takes Root

Despite the entirely innocent and explicable nature of these circumstances, a rumor began to spread within the Muslim community suggesting misconduct between Aisha <span className="font-ar">رضي الله عنها</span> and Safwan <span className="font-ar">رضي الله عنه</span> during this period of separation from the main army.

The sources identify Abdullah ibn Ubayy as playing the leading and most prominent role in initiating and actively spreading this slander. We have encountered Abdullah ibn Ubayy repeatedly throughout this section — his resentment over the kingship that had nearly been his before the arrival of Islam, examined in Unit 16; his damaging withdrawal of a third of the Muslim army during the march to Uhud, examined in Unit 17; and his encouragement of Banu al-Nadir's ultimately unsuccessful defiance, examined in Unit 18. This incident represents perhaps the most personally vicious and calculated of his documented actions against the Muslim community, exploiting an entirely innocent set of circumstances to inflict maximum damage on the Prophet's ﷺ own household and personal standing.

## A Period of Genuine Distress

The effect of this rumor's spread was severe and genuinely distressing for everyone involved. Aisha <span className="font-ar">رضي الله عنها</span> herself fell seriously ill during this period, and by her own later account, remained largely unaware of the full extent and severity of what was being said about her for some time, noticing only that the Prophet's ﷺ usual warmth toward her during her illness seemed somewhat diminished, without understanding why.

The broader Muslim community experienced genuine division during this period as well. Even some sincere believers found themselves uncertain how to respond to the rumors circulating, creating a period of considerable tension and distress that extended for approximately a month. This was, without question, one of the most difficult personal and communal crises the young Muslim community faced during the entire Medinan period — a genuine test of faith, patience, and trust that would ultimately require nothing less than direct divine revelation to resolve completely and decisively.
