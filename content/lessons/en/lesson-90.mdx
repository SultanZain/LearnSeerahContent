## A Judgment Requested and Rendered

Having examined in the previous lesson how Banu Qurayza's own request led to Sa'd ibn Mu'adh <span className="font-ar">رضي الله عنه</span> being entrusted with determining their judgment following their surrender, we turn now to the specific judgment he rendered and the basis on which he stated he was rendering it.

Sa'd ibn Mu'adh <span className="font-ar">رضي الله عنه</span>, brought before the community despite being severely wounded — a wound sustained, notably, during the very Battle of the Trench that Banu Qurayza's breach of covenant had helped make so dangerous, a wound that would eventually prove fatal to him — delivered his determination with clarity and without equivocation.

{/* page */}

## The Judgment and Its Stated Basis

His judgment was that the combatant men of Banu Qurayza should face execution, that the women and children should be taken as captives, and that the tribe's property should be distributed among the Muslim community.

Critically, he did not present this as an arbitrary personal preference or an act of vengeance for his own recent and severe wound. He stated explicitly that this judgment was consistent with the established law found within the Torah itself, regarding the specific circumstance of a besieged people who had broken an existing covenant during an active state of war.

This detail deserves careful attention. Sa'd ibn Mu'adh <span className="font-ar">رضي الله عنه</span> was not inventing a novel standard specifically calibrated to be harsh toward this particular community. He was applying a recognized legal principle from within the very scriptural and legal tradition that Banu Qurayza themselves held as authoritative — the tradition of the Torah, which we examined in detail across Section 1 when studying the Prophet Musa <span className="font-ar">عليه السلام</span> and the legal framework revealed to him.

## The Prophet's ﷺ Confirmation

Upon hearing this judgment, the Prophet ﷺ confirmed it, stating that Sa'd ibn Mu'adh <span className="font-ar">رضي الله عنه</span> had ruled according to the judgment of Allah.

This confirmation carries significant weight for how the episode should be understood. The Prophet ﷺ, who throughout every episode we have examined in this section demonstrated a consistent pattern of measured and proportionate response — the leniency shown toward Banu Qaynuqa, the restraint shown even toward Abdullah ibn Ubayy despite his repeated and damaging conduct, the extended twenty-five day siege that allowed time for surrender rather than immediate assault — affirmed this particular judgment as just and consistent with divine law, applied to this specific and severe circumstance of wartime covenant-breaking.

## Why the Legal Basis Matters

It is worth pausing to consider carefully why the specific grounding of this judgment in Torah law, rather than an arbitrary Islamic innovation, matters so significantly for a fair and honest assessment of this episode.

Banu Qurayza's own tradition, the very scriptural framework their community recognized as authoritative, contained established legal provisions addressing exactly this circumstance: a fortified people who had entered into covenant, subsequently broken that covenant by allying with an existential external threat during active siege warfare, and were ultimately defeated and captured as a result. Sa'd ibn Mu'adh <span className="font-ar">رضي الله عنه</span>, in applying this specific standard, was not subjecting Banu Qurayza to a foreign or unfamiliar system of judgment imposed upon them by an outside power. He was applying, with the confirmation of the Prophet ﷺ himself, a standard rooted in their own recognized legal tradition, to a circumstance that tradition specifically addressed.

This context does not make the judgment any less severe in its actual human consequences, and this curriculum does not present it as though severity itself requires no acknowledgment. But it establishes decisively that this was not an act of arbitrary cruelty or religiously motivated persecution disconnected from any recognized legal framework. It was the application of an established and mutually recognized legal principle to a specific, documented, and extremely serious breach of covenant during an existential wartime crisis.

## A Judgment That Requires Further Context

Having now examined both the documented breach that necessitated this judgment, in the previous lesson, and the specific judgment itself along with its recognized legal basis, in this lesson, this curriculum recognizes that this episode remains one of the more difficult and sometimes contested moments in the entire Seerah when discussed without its full and necessary context.

Contemporary discussions of this episode sometimes present it in isolation — removed from the documented breach of covenant, removed from the existential nature of the Trench crisis that this breach had endangered, removed from the specific legal basis Sa'd ibn Mu'adh <span className="font-ar">رضي الله عنه</span> cited from Banu Qurayza's own tradition, and removed from Banu Qurayza's own choice to request this particular arbiter for their judgment.
