## An Unwelcome Approach

As the Prophet ﷺ and his more than one thousand companions رضي الله عنهم made their way toward Mecca, word of their approach reached the leadership of Quraysh. Despite the explicitly peaceful and unarmed character of this expedition, which we examined carefully in the previous lesson, Quraysh's response reflected genuine alarm rather than acceptance.

They viewed the approach of such a substantial group, even one openly declaring peaceful intentions, as a challenge that could not simply be permitted without response. Allowing over a thousand Muslims to enter the city that had persecuted them for years, regardless of the stated purpose, carried implications for Quraysh's authority and control that their leadership was unwilling to accept without resistance.

## Mobilizing to Block the Path

Quraysh mobilized their own forces to physically prevent the Muslim community from proceeding further toward the city, positioning themselves to block the route that would have taken the Prophet ﷺ and his companions رضي الله عنهم directly to the Masjid al-Haram.

## The Camel That Would Not Move

As the Prophet ﷺ sought an alternate route around Quraysh's blocking forces, his camel Qaswa suddenly knelt to the ground and refused to proceed any further, despite the encouragement of those around her.

The companions رضي الله عنهم present suggested this might simply be the camel's stubbornness, a common enough occurrence with these animals. The Prophet ﷺ responded with a different interpretation entirely:

*"She is not being stubborn, for that is not her nature. Rather, she has been restrained by the One who restrained the elephant."*

This statement connects directly and deliberately back to the story we examined in detail in Section 2 — Abraha's army and the elephant that refused to advance toward Mecca, restrained by the same divine power that would send the birds to destroy that army entirely. By invoking this connection, the Prophet ﷺ framed this moment not as a mere practical obstacle, but as a meaningful sign consistent with Allah's continued protection over the sacred sanctuary of Mecca.

## Making Camp at Hudaybiyyah

With this sign understood, the Prophet ﷺ made the decision to halt the entire expedition and make camp at a location known as Hudaybiyyah, just outside the boundaries of Mecca's sacred territory.

He addressed his companions رضي الله عنهم directly regarding his intentions moving forward: *"By the One in whose hand is my soul, I will accept any proposal they offer me that honors the sanctity of Allah, as long as it does not require me to abandon what Allah has commanded."*

This statement reveals something significant about the Prophet's ﷺ approach throughout this entire episode. Despite commanding a substantial force of over a thousand companions رضي الله عنهم — a number that, while unequipped for battle, was not insignificant had circumstances demanded a different response — his consistent and genuine preference remained peaceful resolution over confrontation, so long as any negotiated outcome did not require compromising the fundamental religious purpose that had brought them on this journey.

## The Search for Water and the First Signs of Difficulty

The camp at Hudaybiyyah faced an immediate practical challenge: the location had limited water available for the substantial number of people and animals now gathered there. The Prophet ﷺ addressed this difficulty directly, and the narrations describe water flowing abundantly from among his own fingers when placed in a nearly empty vessel, sufficient to provide for the entire assembled company — an episode that, alongside the sign of the kneeling camel, reinforced among the companions رضي الله عنهم present a sense that this entire journey, despite its unexpected obstacles, remained under Allah's continued care and guidance.

## Opening Channels of Communication

With the Muslim community encamped and Quraysh's forces positioned to prevent any further advance, both sides began the delicate process of exploring whether some form of negotiated resolution might be possible.

Several messengers and representatives moved between the two camps during this tense period. Quraysh sent individuals to assess Muslim intentions and communicate their own concerns, while the Prophet ﷺ likewise sought to convey clearly that his purpose remained peaceful pilgrimage rather than any form of military threat.

Among the most significant of these exchanges was the specific mission undertaken by Uthman ibn Affan <span className="font-ar">رضي الله عنه</span>, sent by the Prophet ﷺ directly into Mecca itself to communicate with Quraysh's leadership. This particular mission, and the considerable anxiety it generated within the Muslim camp when Uthman <span className="font-ar">رضي الله عنه</span> did not return as quickly as expected, deserves careful and direct examination — particularly given certain claims sometimes made regarding his conduct during this period that this curriculum addresses with full historical evidence in our next lesson.

## A Tense and Uncertain Standoff

As the negotiations continued and messengers moved between the two camps, the atmosphere at Hudaybiyyah remained one of genuine tension and uncertainty. Neither side knew with confidence how this standoff would ultimately resolve, and the specific events that unfolded during this period — including Uthman's <span className="font-ar">رضي الله عنه</span> mission and the remarkable pledge that followed when concern grew for his safety — form the critical turning point of this entire episode, which we examine directly in the lessons that follow.
