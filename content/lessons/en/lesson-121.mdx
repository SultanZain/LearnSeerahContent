## The Peace That Had Held

We examined in considerable detail throughout Unit 23 how the Treaty of Hudaybiyyah, despite its unfavorable appearance at the moment of signing, produced two years of genuine and unprecedented peace between the Muslim community and Quraysh — a peace that allowed the Prophet ﷺ to direct sustained attention toward the diplomatic outreach examined in Unit 24, toward resolving the long-standing threat from Khaybar examined in the previous unit, and toward the broader growth and strengthening of the Muslim community that this period of stability made possible.

This peace, however genuinely beneficial it had proven for the Muslim community, was not destined to last the full ten years its terms had originally specified.

## The Attack on Banu Khuza'ah

The specific breach that would ultimately end this period of peace involved an attack by Banu Bakr, a tribe allied with Quraysh, against Banu Khuza'ah, a tribe that had allied itself with the Muslim community under the specific terms and provisions of the Treaty of Hudaybiyyah.

This attack alone might have been treated as a matter between the two tribes directly involved, but the situation was compounded significantly by credible reports that Quraysh themselves had provided weapons and other material support for this attack against the Muslim community's own ally — a direct and unambiguous violation of the treaty's specific provisions regarding the mutual protection each party owed to its respective allies.

## Abu Sufyan's Desperate Mission

Recognizing the serious danger this breach posed to the continued peace between Quraysh and the Muslim community, Abu Sufyan ibn Harb — the same senior Quraysh leader whose questioning by Heraclius we examined in detail in Unit 24, and whose family connection to the Prophet ﷺ through his daughter's marriage we examined in Unit 21 — personally traveled to Medina in an attempt to renew and strengthen the treaty before the Muslim community could formulate and execute any formal response to the breach.

His mission proved unsuccessful in several respects, including a small but genuinely telling personal detail preserved in the historical record. Upon arriving at his daughter Umm Habibah's <span className="font-ar">رضي الله عنها</span> quarters, he attempted to sit upon the Prophet's ﷺ own bedding, and she declined to permit this, given that he remained, at that point, an unconverted opponent of the very faith and household he was now visiting. This small domestic moment reflects, in a genuinely human and personal way, the significant transformation that had occurred within Abu Sufyan's own family, even as he himself had not yet accepted the message his daughter had embraced years earlier.

## Released From the Treaty's Terms

With Quraysh's breach of the treaty now clearly and demonstrably established, a fundamental principle of treaty relations — recognized broadly across the ancient world and remaining a foundational principle of international relations to this day — came into effect: a violation by one party releases the other party from continued adherence to terms that the violating party has itself abandoned.

This meant the Prophet ﷺ was no longer bound by the specific obligations of the Hudaybiyyah agreement, freeing him to respond to this breach, and to the broader and extended history of conflict between the Muslim community and Quraysh that we have traced across nearly every unit of this entire section, in ways that would not have been available to him while the treaty remained genuinely and mutually honored by both parties.

## Setting the Stage for What Followed

This breach, arriving after two years of genuine peace during which the Muslim community had grown considerably in strength, numbers, and organizational capacity exactly as we examined throughout Unit 23, provided the specific and immediate circumstance that would lead directly to one of the most significant and consequential events in the entire history of early Islam: the Prophet's ﷺ march on Mecca itself.

## What Remains Ahead

The events that would follow this breach — the Prophet's ﷺ decision to march on Mecca, the remarkable diplomatic developments that preceded the city's fall, and above all the extraordinary act of mercy and reconciliation that the Prophet ﷺ would extend to the very city and leadership that had persecuted him and his followers for over two decades — represent perhaps the single most significant demonstration of the character and mission this curriculum has traced consistently since our earliest lessons in Section 1.

We turn to this remarkable conquest directly in the unit ahead, examining how a moment that could easily have become an occasion for justified retribution instead became one of the most profound and historically significant examples of forgiveness and reconciliation in the entire history of human civilization.
