## The Final Afternoon

On a Monday — the same day of the week on which he had been born, examined in Section 2, and the same day of the week on which the first revelation had descended, examined in Section 4 — the Prophet ﷺ passed from this world, in the heat of a Medina afternoon, within the quarters he had chosen for his final days.

## His Final Words

Aisha <span className="font-ar">رضي الله عنها</span>, whose careful and detailed preservation of this most intimate and significant moment has given the entire Muslim community access to its final details, described the Prophet's ﷺ final words with particular care.

He is reported to have spoken words expressing a choice for the highest and most exalted companionship, echoing directly a passage revealed earlier in the Quran:

<Quran surah="4" ayah="69" />

*"And whoever obeys Allah and the Messenger — those will be with the ones upon whom Allah has bestowed favor: of the Prophets, the truthful, the martyrs, and the righteous. And excellent are those as companions."*

These final words reflect a peaceful and complete awareness of what was occurring — not resistance or fear, but a conscious and willing acceptance of the moment that had finally arrived, choosing the eternal companionship of the very Prophets and righteous believers this entire curriculum has traced throughout its examination of sacred history.

As news of this profound loss spread through Medina, Umar ibn al-Khattab <span className="font-ar">رضي الله عنه</span> — whose formidable directness and strength of conviction this curriculum has traced consistently throughout every section since his dramatic conversion examined in Section 4 — reacted with genuine and overwhelming disbelief, reportedly threatening anyone who dared to claim the Prophet ﷺ had actually died.

He recited to the assembled community the same verse we examined in Section 4 in connection with the false report of the Prophet's ﷺ death at Uhud:

## The Completion of an Entire Theological Arc

This moment, examined with the full reverence and care it deserves, completes directly the theological framework this entire curriculum established at its very beginning in Section 1. We recall our extensive examination there of the nature of Prophethood itself — that every Prophet, without exception, was a genuine human being, subject to the same mortality every human being faces, however elevated and honored their specific mission and standing before Allah.

Muhammad ﷺ, the final and universal Messenger whose life this curriculum has traced across every section since that theological foundation, was no exception to this same principle. His death did not diminish the truth or completeness of the message he had delivered — a completeness confirmed explicitly through the declaration at Arafat examined in the previous unit. The Quran and Sunnah, perfected and preserved, would continue to guide the Muslim community across every subsequent generation, exactly as we examined when studying the finality of Prophethood in Section 1, regardless of the Prophet's ﷺ own continued physical presence among his community.
