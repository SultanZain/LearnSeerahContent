## A Mission of Trust, Not Flight

## Chosen Directly by the Prophet ﷺ

Uthman ibn Affan <span className="font-ar">رضي الله عنه</span> did not choose this mission for himself, nor did he flee toward Mecca to escape any responsibility. He was selected specifically and deliberately by the Prophet ﷺ himself as his personal representative and envoy, sent on direct prophetic command into a city that remained actively hostile to the Muslim community.

The Prophet's ﷺ reasoning for this specific selection reflected careful strategic consideration. Uthman <span className="font-ar">رضي الله عنه</span> belonged to a branch of Quraysh that carried certain connections and standing within the city that might afford him a measure of access and protection that other companions رضي الله عنهم, without similar family ties, may not have received when entering a city that had been in active conflict with the Muslim community for years.

## The Task He Was Given

Uthman's <span className="font-ar">رضي الله عنه</span> mission was clear and diplomatically significant: to communicate directly to Quraysh's leadership that the Muslim community had come specifically and only to perform the rites of Umrah, to honor the sanctity of the Kaaba, and not for any purpose of warfare or conflict — and to explore, on the Prophet's ﷺ behalf, whether terms could be negotiated allowing their peaceful entry.

This was a mission requiring genuine courage. Uthman <span className="font-ar">رضي الله عنه</span> entered a city that had persecuted the Muslim community for years, that had organized military campaigns against Medina that we examined throughout this entire section, carrying a message on behalf of the very man Quraysh had spent over a decade trying to silence or destroy.

Uthman's <span className="font-ar">رضي الله عنه</span> mission into Mecca took longer than anticipated, and as the days passed without his return, genuine concern began to spread through the Muslim camp at Hudaybiyyah.

Given the genuinely tense and hostile atmosphere that had characterized the relationship between the two communities throughout this entire section, this was not an unreasonable fear — it reflected the real and material danger that any Muslim envoy entering Mecca during this period genuinely faced.
